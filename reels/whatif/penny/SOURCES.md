# Sources (read 16 November 2026)

Natalie Wolchover, "Could a Penny Dropped Off a Skyscraper Actually Kill You?", Life's Little Mysteries, 5 March 2012,
republished by Scientific American (scientificamerican.com/article/could-a-penny-dropped-off/) and NBC News.
Building: esbnyc.com/about/facts-figures ("1,454 feet to the top of the building's antenna").

- Line 1 (the question) and the answer: "it's extremely difficult to turn a penny into a lethal weapon."
- Line 3: pennies are "flat, so they experience a lot of air resistance, and they are light, so it doesn't take much drag to counteract their weight."
- Line 4: terminal velocity is reached after "only about 50 feet (15 meters) of descent."
- Line 5: "they flutter to the ground at a measly 25 mph (40 kph)"; it "would flutter to the ground, like a leaf."
- Line 6: Louis Bloomfield, University of Virginia, "used wind tunnels and helium balloons to replicate the fall of pennies from skyscrapers"; "I think one bounced off my face once."
- Line 7: "it would feel like being flicked in the forehead".
- Line 8: pens "will either spin and flutter, or shoot down like an arrow"; "it might well come down at 200 mph"; "It could punch into a wooden board."
- Line 2 states a common belief, which the film then corrects; it is not presented as fact.
