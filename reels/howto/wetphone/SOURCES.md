# Sources (Apple Support, read 15 November 2026)

Main source: "If you see a liquid-detection alert on your iPhone", support.apple.com/en-us/102643.
Supporting: "About splash, water, and dust resistance of iPhone 7 and later", support.apple.com/en-us/108039.

- Lines 1-2: "Don't put your iPhone in a bag of rice. Doing so could allow small particles of rice to damage your iPhone." 102643
- Line 3: "Don't plug the cable in again until your iPhone and the cable are completely dry." 102643. "do not charge your device until it's completely dry." 108039
- Line 4: "the pins on the connector or cable can corrode and cause permanent damage or stop functioning" 102643
- Line 5: "Don't dry your iPhone using an external heat source or compressed air." 102643 (hair dryer and heater are examples of an external heat source)
- Line 6: "Don't insert a foreign object, such as a cotton swab or a paper towel, into the connector." 102643
- Line 7: "unplug all cables" 108039; "wipe it off with a soft, lint-free cloth." 108039
- Line 8: "Tap your iPhone gently against your hand with the connector facing down to remove excess liquid." 102643
- Line 9: "Leave your iPhone in a dry area with some airflow." 102643
- Line 10: "After at least 30 minutes, try charging"; "It might take up to 24 hours to fully dry." 102643

Limits: this is Apple's guidance for iPhone. Samsung's and Google's pages could not be read (404 or unrelated pages), so the
description will say to follow your own phone maker's instructions. Left out: wireless charging (Apple allows it once the back is
dry, but that is iPhone-specific), the emergency charging override (iPhone-specific), salt water and rinsing (not on the pages read).
