# Sources (checked November 2026)

- Speed: "Channel rip currents typically flow about 1 to 2 feet per second and may reach speeds up to 8 feet per second" and the Olympic-swimmer comparison: US National Weather Service, weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent-science; NOAA, oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/ripcurrent.html
- Does not pull you under: NWS, same page ("neither will pull a person under the water").
- Width: NWS, same page ("typically range in width from 5 to 100 yards").
- Do not fight it, swim parallel, return at an angle; fatigue is the danger: NOAA, same page.
- The calm, darker gap between breaking waves: NWS, same page.
- Line 7: "If you feel you can't reach shore, relax, face the shore, and call or wave for help": NWS, weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent-surviving. The same page: "Relax. Rip currents don't pull you under" and "Swim along the shoreline until you escape the current's pull".
- Lines 8-9 (someone else is caught): "Alert a lifeguard", "Throw them something that floats", "DO NOT enter the water yourself; many rip current fatalities occur from someone trying to rescue others": NOAA National Hurricane Center, nhc.noaa.gov/rip-currents/survive.html
- Last line: "If at all possible, only swim at beaches with lifeguards": NWS, weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent-surviving
