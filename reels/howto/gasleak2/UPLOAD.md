## Description
That rotten-egg smell is added to gas so you notice a leak, and one small spark can set it off. Do not turn anything on or off: no lights, no appliances, not even the doorbell. Do not strike a match or light a candle, and do not go looking for the leak.

Get everyone out right away. Do not use your phone until you are well away from the building, then call your gas company or your emergency services. Stay out until they tell you it is safe. If the smell is strong or you are not sure, leave first and call second.

Sources:
Connecticut Public Utilities Regulatory Authority, What To Do If You Smell Natural Gas: https://portal.ct.gov/PURA/Gas-Pipeline-Safety/What-To-Do-If--You-Smell-Natural-Gas

Pacific Gas and Electric, gas safety: https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/safety/gas-safety.html

General safety information, not professional training. Follow your own gas company's instructions. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
