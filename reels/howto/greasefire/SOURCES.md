# Sources (checked November 2026)

- No water, and why: "Never pour water on a cooking pan grease fire"; water thrown into burning pans "actually intensified and spread the fires": Cambridge (Massachusetts) Fire Department, cambridgema.gov/cfd/News/2023/05/cookingfiresafety. "Never pour water on a grease fire": City of Longmont, longmontcolorado.gov/news/what-to-do-if-you-have-a-cooking-fire/. London Fire Brigade's pan-fire page gives the fireball as the reason (read as a summary, not verbatim).
- Do not move the pan; turn off the burner: "Turn off the burner. Do not move the pan.": Cambridge FD, same page; Longmont, same page.
- Lid or baking sheet: "Keep a pan lid or baking sheet nearby. Use it to cover the pan if it catches on fire.": US Fire Administration, usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/. "smother the flames by sliding the lid over the pan": Cambridge FD.
- Leave it covered: "leave the lid over the pan until the pan has cooled" ("To keep the fire from restarting"): Cambridge FD; "until the pan is completely cool": Longmont.
- Get out: "When in doubt, just get out. When you leave close the door behind you to help contain the fire. Call 911.": Longmont.
- Unattended cooking: "The leading factor contributing to ignition in nonconfined home cooking fires was unattended equipment (37%)"; "Stand by your pan.": US Fire Administration, same page.
- Not used because no page we read supports it: baking soda, flour, fire blankets, the steam explanation of why water spreads the fire.
