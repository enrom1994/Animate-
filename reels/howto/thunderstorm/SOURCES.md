# Sources (US National Weather Service, read 14 November 2026)

- Line 1: "Never shelter under an isolated tree." weather.gov/safety/lightning-tips. "Being underneath a tree is the second leading cause of lightning casualties." weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths
- Line 2: a side flash is when "lightning strikes a taller object near the victim and a portion of the current jumps from taller object to the victim"; "Most often, side flash victims have taken shelter under a tree". weather.gov/safety/lightning-struck
- Line 3: "Never lie flat on the ground." lightning-tips. "Lying flat increases your chance of being affected by potentially deadly ground current." lightning-myths
- Line 4: "Crouching doesn't make you any safer outdoors." lightning-myths
- Line 5: "If you hear thunder, lightning is close enough to strike you." lightning-tips
- Line 6: "a substantial building with electricity or plumbing or an enclosed, metal-topped vehicle with windows up." lightning-tips
- Line 7: "a tent offers NO protection"; "stay off porches"; "Picnic shelters, dugouts and small buildings without plumbing or electricity are not safe." weather.gov/safety/lightning-outdoors, lightning-tips, weather.gov/safety/lightning-indoors
- Line 8: "Immediately get off elevated areas such as hills"; "get out and away from ponds, lakes and other bodies of water"; "Stay away from objects that conduct electricity (barbed wire fences, power lines, windmills, etc.)". lightning-tips
- Line 9: "If you are in a group, spread out to avoid the current traveling between group members." lightning-outdoors
- Line 10: "Stay in safe shelter at least 30 minutes after you hear the last sound of thunder." lightning-tips
- Line 11: "The human body does not store electricity. It is perfectly safe to touch a lightning victim to give them first aid." lightning-myths. "calling 911, starting CPR, and using an AED" lightning-struck
