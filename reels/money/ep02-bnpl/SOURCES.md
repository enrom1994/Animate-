# Ep. 2 "Buy now, pay later": sources

Checked 21 November 2026. US figures. Every on-screen number is either sourced below or marked illustrative.

| Claim in the script | Source | Exact figure |
|---|---|---|
| Nearly half of users paid late this past year | LendingTree BNPL tracker, updated 17 Nov 2026: https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/buy-now-pay-later-loan-statistics/ (QuestionPro online survey of 2,060 US consumers aged 18 to 80, 17 to 23 March 2026) | "47% of users say they've paid late in the past year, up from 41% in 2025 and 34% in 2024." 72% of late payers say their latest late payment was no more than about a week late. A self-reported, nonprobability survey: the film says "users", not "Americans" |
| One in four has had three or more loans at once | Same LendingTree page | "25% who've held three or more at once" (23% the year before); 63% have held more than one at once |
| Stacking is common (backs the story, not spoken as a number) | CFPB, 13 Jan 2025, data for 2022: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-research-reveals-heavy-buy-now-pay-later-use-among-borrowers-with-high-credit-balances-and-multiple-pay-in-four-loans | "Approximately 63 percent of borrowers originated multiple simultaneous loans at some point during the year"; 33% used more than one lender. Lender data, so it independently backs the survey's 63% |
| Shoppers spend about 10% more when they can split the price | Maesen & Ang, "Buy Now Pay Later: Impact of Installment Payments on Customer Purchases", Journal of Marketing (2024), DOI 10.1177/00222429241282414; summary by the AMA, 4 Feb 2025: https://ama.org/2025/02/04/buy-now-pay-later-increases-customer-spending | About 9% higher purchase incidence and about 10% larger purchase amounts, one major US retailer, effect lasting the 26 weeks studied. Spoken as "one large study", because it is one retailer |
| Four payments, every two weeks | NerdWallet reviews of Klarna and Afterpay, updated 18 Nov 2026: https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/klarna-buy-now-pay-later , https://www.nerdwallet.com/personal-loans/reviews/afterpay-buy-now-pay-later | Pay in 4: the first payment at checkout, three more two weeks apart, no interest |
| Late fee (on screen: "LATE FEE · UP TO $8 AFTER 10 DAYS"; spoken: "One bounces two days before payday. Ten days later, a late fee.") | Afterpay US Installment Agreement, last updated September 2026: https://www.afterpay.com/en-US/installment-agreement (read 23 Nov 2026) | Section 4: the late fee applies if an installment "remains unpaid for a period of ten (10) days after the due date", "up to a maximum of $8.00"; total late fees "will not exceed 25% of the order value"; none for Rhode Island residents. Klarna, PayPal and Affirm pages could not be read (blocked or empty); NerdWallet (18 Nov 2026) reports Klarna up to $7 after 10 days overdue. The first cut said a payment two days late brought a fee: wrong under these terms, corrected |
| Plans charge no interest | Same NerdWallet reviews; CFPB report above | Pay in 4 is interest-free; longer monthly plans can charge interest and are not what this episode shows |

## Illustrative (shown as plain prices, not claims)
- Sneakers $400 (4 × $100), phone $600 (4 × $150), console $500 (4 × $125): $375 due every two weeks once all three run.
- "Two days before payday": the story's timing, not a statistic.

## Said plainly
- The late fee is small. The film does not claim pay-later ruins anyone; its point is that it makes buying easy and payments pile up.
- "Education, not financial advice" in the description.

## Left out
- Credit-card interest if Max covers a payment with his card: true to life, but it needs its own sourced APR and makes the film longer.
- Credit-score effects: lenders differ (some now report to credit bureaus, some do not).
