# Pip & Biscuit: "Why Do Cats Sleep So Much?" — Crayon Picture Book test

A 9:16 Short (1080×1920, ~55 s, 24 fps) for the Pip & Biscuit kids channel, in the Lemo-Opuscar **Crayon Picture Book** style.
Purpose: test whether a second look (hand-drawn crayon) works for the channel, next to the stick-figure episodes.
Narrator: Pip (the channel voice: Kokoro af_heart, +4 semitones, 1.0x). Language: English.

## Three candidate structures

1. **A day on one page** — the page is drawn live as the day goes by: morning sun, Biscuit asleep in three places, a row of 24 hour-boxes coloured in, dusk zoomies, then the night wash reveals white-wax stars and a dream. Ending echoes the opening ("Asleep. Again.").
2. **A flip through three pages** — sofa, sunbeam, sock drawer, each a page turn, with a fact per page. Clear, but the facts become a list and the wash has no reason to arrive.
3. **The clock page** — a giant crayon clock; Biscuit's sleep coloured in as wedges. Strong for the number, weak for story and character.

**Chosen: 1.** One page, one day: the passing of time *is* the topic, and the style's native move (a night wash revealing hidden white crayon) lands exactly on the emotional peak, the dream. The hour-box row from (3) survives as one beat inside it, because "colouring is an action": colouring 16 boxes is the fact.

## Logline and arc

Pip wonders why Biscuit is always asleep; over one crayon day we learn why, and at nightfall the page reveals what she might be dreaming.
- **Setup**: Biscuit asleep, again, everywhere (gag: the sock drawer).
- **Turn**: why? Wild cats hunted for every meal; rest saves energy for the big moments. Even asleep, her ears listen. At sunset she wakes: zoom!
- **Ending**: night wash, white-wax stars appear, a dream bubble, and Biscuit is asleep. Again.

## Benchmark
Learn: picture-book tenderness, music-led pacing, held frames that breathe (boil). Don't learn: any specific book's characters, songs or images.

## Script (printed words = subtitles)

1. This is my cat, Biscuit. She's asleep. Again.
2. Asleep on the sofa. Asleep in the sun. Asleep in my sock drawer!
3. Cats sleep about twelve to sixteen hours a day.  *(subtitle: 12 to 16)*
4. That's half the day, or even more!
5. Why so sleepy? Long ago, wild cats had to hunt for every meal.
6. Running and pouncing takes lots of energy.
7. So cats rest a lot, and save it for the big moments.
8. Most cat naps are light. Even asleep, her ears keep listening.
9. Cats are busiest at sunrise and sunset. Like right now!
10. Zoom!
11. And when the stars come out...
12. Scientists think cats might even dream.
13. Goodnight, Biscuit. Asleep. Again.

### Fact notes
- 12–16 h/day: the commonly cited range for adult cats (veterinary sources, e.g. Cornell Feline Health Center, PetMD); kittens and seniors sleep more. "Half the day, or even more" is exact for 12–16 of 24.
- Energy for hunting: the usual explanation for cats' long rest (short bursts of high-energy hunting); phrased as background, not a lab result.
- Light sleep: most cat sleep is light dozing; ears still respond to sounds (commonly described; hedged as "most").
- Crepuscular: cats are typically most active around dawn and dusk.
- Dreaming: cats have REM sleep (documented since Jouvet's 1960s work on cats); "scientists think cats might even dream" is the honest hedge.

## Cue map / score
- 92 bpm, 6/8 lilt, D major. Ukulele + clarinet (bass, humour) + glockenspiel; music box for the night.
- Sections: morning (uke + clarinet), count (glock note per box over a soft uke), long ago (bassoon, woodblock steps), listening (music drops to near-silence), sunset zoom (shaker, fast uke strum, clarinet run), night (music box alone, then the full theme once, softly).
- Two silences: before the ear clink, and a breath before the wash.
