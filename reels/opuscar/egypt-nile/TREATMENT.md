# "Why does almost everyone in Egypt live on 4% of the land?" — Satellite Briefing test

A 9:16 Short (1080×1920, ~55 s, 30 fps) for a faceless geography channel, in the Lemo-Opuscar **Satellite Briefing** style.
Narrator: a calm analyst (Kokoro `bm_george`). Language: English. Facts and sources: `FACTS.md`.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Day and night, one locked frame** — Egypt by day (a brown country with a green thread), a scan pass turns it to night and only the thread lights up. Then dive into the valley, measure its width, climb to the delta and Cairo, climb back to orbit.
2. **A river followed from source to mouth** — Aswan → Luxor → Cairo → the sea, a reading at each stop. Clear, but slow for 55 s and the population claim arrives late.
3. **Two places at one scale** — Egypt vs France + Germany, whip-pan comparison. Strong hook, but the story is the Nile, not the comparison.

**Chosen: 1.** The night-lights reveal is the hook and the proof in one image; the comparison from (3) survives as the first line only (no map of Europe on screen — no borders needed). The scan from day to night is the style's time move, used for *time of day* instead of years.

## Logline and arc
From orbit Egypt looks like a big desert country; at night it is one bright line. Setup: size. Turn: the night pass. Proof: a measured width of the green strip. Ending: the delta and Cairo, then the climb back to orbit with the line still glowing.

## Score & sound
Dry telemetry palette: FM lock-on ping, grid ticks, noise scan for the day→night pass, servo whine on the dive, a ratchet on the range line, timpani + low brass on the two key readings (95%, the width). Pulse bass with harmonics, detuned pad under a low-pass. One wet timbre (glass) marks the turn (night). A suck-out before the width reading lands. Voice ~10 dB over music, −14 LUFS.
